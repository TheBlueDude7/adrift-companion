# Adrift: Mechs

Mechs
Mechs are wearable expedition platforms built to carry human beings into environments their bodies were never meant to survive.
They are not vehicles in the traditional sense. A mech moves with its pilot, enclosing or supporting the body through powered limbs, reinforced plating, environmental seals, and modular equipment.Mechs are strongest when the crew understands the mission ahead.
A frame configured to resist Chemical threats and transport biological samples may be invaluable in an infested laboratory. The same frame may become loud, cumbersome, and dangerously vulnerable when the expedition encounters an electrical predator in a narrow maintenance shaft.
Mechs provide modularity.
They trade fuel, space, stealth, and access to bodily upgrades for protection and mission-specific capability.
Mech Storage
Ordinary storage aboard the ship is not tracked. The crew may store common equipment, materials, and supplies without counting Cargo Slots.
Mechs are an exception.
An assembled mech requires a dedicated maintenance berth, charging connection, and enough open space to enter, repair, and reconfigure it.
Every ship can support one assembled mech by default.
Cargo Bay upgrades increase the number of assembled mechs the ship can support.

The following count against Mech Capacity:
Operational mechs.
Damaged mechs.
Offline mechs.
Partially repaired assembled frames.
Captured or recovered intact mech frames.
The following do not count:
Mech modules stored separately.
Ammunition.
Replacement plating.
Dismantled mech components.
Blueprints.
Unassembled construction materials.
A mech exceeding the ship’s capacity must be:
Left outside the ship.
Secured externally.
Stored aboard another vessel.
Completely dismantled.
Abandoned.
A mech secured outside the ship may be damaged or lost during travel, collisions, hazardous tiles, or ship combat.

Completing the Frame
When the Project reaches its required Progress:
Pay the listed Completion Cost.
Record the mech’s chassis and Tier.
Calculate its Maximum Mech HP.
Record its Frame Strength and Frame Constitution.
Choose any built-in Tier Resistance.
Record its Module Slots and Hardpoints.
Record its Frame Armor.
Add the mech to the ship’s Mech Capacity.
Install completed modules using normal installation Tasks.
A newly completed mech begins at full HP, with its frame plating intact, and with no optional modules installed.

Frame Armor
Every frame is plated, and the heavier the Tier the thicker it is: 4 Armor Points at Tier I, 6 at Tier II, and 8 at Tier III. Defensive modules add their own Armor on top of this.
Frame Armor is ablative, exactly like a survivor’s. Each hit it absorbs stops 1 Damage and costs the plating 1 Point. At 0 Points the plating is spent and stops nothing until it is replaced, though the frame’s maximum never drops: replating restores it in full.
Upgrading a Tier hands the mech the Points its new frame adds, so a Tier I mech with full plating that becomes Tier II has 6.
Using Frame Attributes
While operating a powered mech, the pilot may use the mech’s Frame Strength instead of their own Strength for:
Lifting.
Carrying.
Pushing.
Dragging.
Bracing.
Forced movement.
Mech-mounted Physical attacks.
Operating powered limbs.
Breaking structures.
The pilot may use Frame Constitution instead of their own Constitution for:
Resisting vacuum.
Resisting environmental heat or cold.
Resisting Chemical exposure blocked by the frame.
Remaining functional under acceleration or impact.
Other hazards directly absorbed by the chassis.
Frame Constitution does not protect against:
Psychological effects.
Internal disease.
Existing Poison.
Stress.
Afflictions.
Damage that bypasses the frame.
Hazards affecting the pilot after the frame becomes Breached.
Mech Module Slots
A mech may install a number of modules determined by its Tier.
Modules may provide:
Additional cargo.
Armor.
Damage Resistance.
Environmental protection.
Weapons.
Medical equipment.
Sensors.
Tools.
Movement options.
Condition Resistance or Immunity.
A mech cannot install more modules than it has available slots.
Heavy or unusually powerful modules may consume two slots.
Reconfiguration
Mech modules may be changed aboard the ship.
Under safe conditions, reconfiguring modules does not require a full day segment, but it does require:
An operational mech berth.
Appropriate tools.
Available replacement modules.
A powered ship or external power supply.
Installing experimental, damaged, or unfamiliar modules may require a Skilled Engineering or Mechanical Technician Task.
A mech cannot normally change modules during an expedition.

Mechs and Bodily Upgrades
A mech surrounds the pilot’s body with its own movement systems, armor, sensors, power supply, and weapon interfaces.
This prevents many cybernetic and biological upgrades from functioning normally.
Biological Upgrades
While operating a mech, the pilot cannot activate or benefit from biological upgrades that provide:
Damage bonuses.
Damage-type attacks.
Armor.
Damage Resistance.
Additional Actions.
Extra movement.
Forced movement.
Reactive attacks.
Condition-triggered combat abilities.
Environmental adaptation already replaced by the sealed frame.
The following biological effects remain:
Maximum HP changes.
Permanent Attribute changes.
Replacement organs.
Effects needed to remain alive.
Passive effects that cannot reasonably be suppressed.
Effects explicitly marked Mech-Compatible.
A biological character does not lose their upgrades.
The frame prevents them from using those upgrades effectively while enclosed.
Cybernetic Upgrades
Cybernetics that replace or enhance limbs, Armor, movement, weapons, or bodily defenses are normally disabled while piloting a mech.
Cybernetics may continue functioning when they provide:
Basic life support.
Replacement sensory function.
Neural control.
Communication.
Memory.
Medical regulation.
A function explicitly marked Frame-Compatible.

Upgrade Tags
Cybernetic and biological upgrades may use the following tags:
Mech-Compatible: Functions normally while piloting a mech.
Suppressed: Provides no benefit while piloting a mech.
Interface: Functions only while connected to a mech or other machine.
Replacement: Remains functional because the character requires it for ordinary bodily function.
When an upgrade has no tag, the GM determines whether the mech physically replaces or prevents its use.
Afflictions and Injuries
Mechs do not suppress Afflictions.
They may compensate for Injuries when the frame physically replaces the impaired function.
Examples include:
A mech allows a character with a Broken Leg to move.
A mech allows a character with Spinal Trauma to join an expedition.
A sealed frame supports a character with damaged lungs.
Powered manipulators compensate for a Crushed Limb.
The Injury remains.
If the mech becomes Offline, Power-Starved, Breached, or abandoned, the Injury’s normal effects immediately return.

Deploying a Mech
Preparing a mech for an expedition requires:
One available assembled mech.
One pilot.
Fuel based on the mech’s Tier.
All installed modules recorded.
An operational environmental seal, if required.
Sufficient Mech Capacity aboard the ship.
Fuel is consumed when the mech is deployed.
A deployment normally covers one expedition lasting no more than one day.
A longer expedition may consume additional Fuel at the beginning of each additional day.
Entering and Exiting
Entering or exiting a mech is free under safe conditions.
During combat, entering or exiting a mech requires a Slow Action.
The frame must be:
Accessible.
Open.
Powered enough to release or accept the pilot.
Large enough for the surrounding zone.
A Servo-Locked or Power-Starved mech may require a Skilled Task to exit.
Mech Durability
A mech has its own Maximum HP.
While a character is operating a mech, incoming damage is normally dealt to the mech before it reaches the pilot.
Mech HP is separate from the pilot’s HP.
Damage dealt to a mech does not normally cause the pilot to roll for an Injury.
Resolving Damage Against a Mech
When a mech pilot is attacked, resolve damage in the following order:
Determine the attack’s base Damage.
Apply the mech’s Vulnerabilities.
Apply the mech’s damage Resistances.
Apply Cover.
Apply the mech’s Armor: if any Damage is left and the plating has Points, it stops 1 and loses 1 Point.
Deal the remaining Damage to the mech’s HP.
If the mech is reduced to 0 HP, apply any excess Damage to the pilot.
The pilot’s personal Armor and Resistances may reduce excess Damage normally.
Example
A Tier I Utility Frame has 2 HP remaining.
It is struck by an attack dealing 4 Physical Damage.
The mech has Physical Resistance 1.
The Resistance reduces the attack from 4 Damage to 3.
The mech loses its remaining 2 HP.
The final 1 Damage passes through to the pilot.
The pilot may apply personal Armor or Resistance against that 1 Damage.
Direct Attacks Against the Pilot
Most attacks strike the mech first.
An effect may bypass the mech when it specifically targets:
The pilot’s mind.
Internal biological processes.
Pressure or Stress.
A preexisting Infection or Poison.
An exposed pilot.
A breach in the cockpit.
A psychic, sonic, or similarly penetrating effect.
Another vulnerability explicitly described by the attack.
The attack should clearly state when it bypasses Mech HP.
Ordinary claws, bullets, fire, impacts, and environmental damage strike the mech first.
Mechs at 0 HP
When a mech reaches 0 HP, it becomes Offline.
An Offline mech:
Cannot move under its own power.
Provides no Frame Attributes.
Cannot use powered modules.
Cannot use mounted weapons.
Cannot provide damage Resistances from active systems.
Cannot protect the pilot from further attacks.
May still function as Cover.
May remain environmentally sealed if the attack did not cause a Breach.
The pilot remains inside the frame until they exit.
During combat, exiting an Offline mech requires a Slow Action.
If the controls, cockpit, or release mechanisms are damaged, exiting may require a Skilled Mechanical Technician, Engineering, or Piloting Task.
Excess Damage and Injuries
The mech itself does not suffer Injuries.
The pilot only rolls for an Injury when:
Damage passes through the mech and meets the normal Injury requirements.
An attack bypasses the mech.
The mech’s destruction directly injures the pilot.
A specific ability forces an Injury.
A mech reaching 0 HP may create an immediate complication when appropriate.
Possible complications include:
The mech becomes Breached.
The pilot becomes Staggered.
The pilot is Restrained inside the damaged frame.
The mech falls Prone.
A mounted system catches fire.
The cockpit fills with smoke.
The frame becomes Servo-Locked.

Mech Conditions
Mechs may still suffer conditions independently of HP.
Common conditions include:
Breached.
Conductive.
Corroded.
Disrupted.
Immobilized.
Jammed.
Overheated..
Power-Starved.
Sensor-Blind.
Servo-Locked.
A condition may impair a mech without dealing HP Damage.
For example:
An Electric attack may Disrupt a mech that still has full HP.
Chemical Damage may apply Corroded.
A creature may Immobilize the frame by trapping its legs.
A system failure may make it Power-Starved.
Repairing HP does not automatically remove conditions.
Removing a condition does not automatically restore HP.

Field Repairs
A mech may be repaired during an expedition.
Field repairs are normally Skilled or Crisis Tasks.
A successful field repair:
Restores 2 Mech HP.
Consumes the listed materials and Toolkit charges.
Cannot raise the mech above half its Maximum HP, rounded up.
A field repair performed while enemies are nearby, the mech is exposed to a hazard, or the pilot remains inside is a Crisis Task.
A failed field repair may:
Consume the materials.
Apply Disrupted.
Apply Power-Starved.
Apply Servo-Locked.
Cause the mech to make noise.
Prevent another field-repair attempt until conditions improve.
Full repairs require the ship’s Workshop or mech berth.
Mech Weapons
Mech-mounted weapons do not track ammunition. They do, however, use hardpoints.
A mounted weapon may be used whenever:
The mech is powered.
The weapon module is functional.
The pilot spends the required Action.
The target is within range.
No condition prevents its use.
Weapons use the pilot’s appropriate piloting Skill.
Mounted weapons do not grant the mech a separate turn.
These costs must be listed in the module’s rules.
Unless specifically stated otherwise, ordinary Ballistic, Physical, Fire, Electric, and Chemical mech weapons have no ammunition cost.
Noise and Size
Mechs are larger and louder than ordinary expedition equipment.
Unless a frame or module states otherwise:
Group Stealth suffers -3d6 while a mech is active.
All enemies attacking a character in a mech have 3d6 dice added to the attack roll, due to its size.
A mech cannot enter spaces too small for its chassis.
Weak floors, narrow hatches, ladders, vents, and unstable structures may block it.
Running, breaching, and using Heavy modules may advance Noise Clocks.
Some creatures may detect the mech’s heat, power, vibration, or electronic emissions.
A powered-down mech may be quieter, but becomes Power-Starved until restarted.
Restarting it during danger may require a Slow Action or Skilled Piloting Task.

Crafting Mechs and Mech Modules
Mechs consist of two parts:
A mech frame, which determines the chassis and Tier.
Mech modules, which are installed into the frame’s available Module Slots.
Constructing a complete mech frame is a major Project.
Crafting and installing individual modules are ordinary Tasks.
Modules do not require blueprints, research prerequisites, or separate construction Projects unless a specific rare or experimental module explicitly says otherwise.
Constructing a Mech Frame
A mech frame is too large and complex to fabricate as a single ordinary Task.
Constructing one uses the Project rules from Chapter 2.
The crew chooses:
Utility Frame.
Assault Frame.
Recon Frame.
Mech Tier.
The chassis determines the mech’s basic role.
The Tier determines its:
Maximum HP.
Module Slots.
Frame Strength.
Frame Constitution.
Frame Armor.
Built-in Resistances.
Fuel cost.
The exact Project requirements and material costs are listed with each mech frame.
When the Project is completed, the crew creates one assembled mech.
The mech counts against the ship’s Mech Capacity.
It begins with no optional modules installed.
Crafting Mech Modules
Mech modules are crafted using the normal crafting rules in Chapter 3.
Crafting a module is one shipboard Task.
To craft a module, a character must have:
An operational Workshop of the required Grade.
An appropriate Toolkit.
The required materials.
The required Skill Level or willingness to attempt a Skilled Task.
One available day segment.
The listed Energy.
Any standard mech module may be crafted without first obtaining a blueprint.
Experimental, alien, or unique modules may still have special requirements stated in their individual entries.
Standard Module Costs
All mech modules of the same Tier use the same material cost.
A module’s Slot cost, function, or weapon type does not change its crafting cost during this playtest.

Routine Module Crafting
If the crafter meets the Routine Threshold and has all required materials, tools, and facilities, the module is crafted automatically.
Spend:
The listed Energy.
One day segment.
The required materials.
One Toolkit charge.
The crafter receives Routine Crafting Benefits as described in Chapter 3.
The completed module may be installed immediately or placed into storage.
Finished modules do not count against Mech Capacity.
Skilled Module Crafting
A character who does not meet the Routine Threshold may still attempt to craft the module as a Skilled Task.
Crafting also becomes Skilled when:
The Workshop is Damaged.
The Toolkit is inadequate.
Substitute materials are used.
The character is working during danger.
The module is alien, unstable, or unfamiliar.
Another circumstance creates meaningful uncertainty.
On a success, the module is completed.
Additional successes provide normal Crafting Stunts.
On a failure, use the standard failed-crafting rules from Chapter 3.
Installing Mech Modules
Installing a module is a separate Task from crafting it.
To install a module, the crew must have:
An assembled mech.
A completed module.
Enough available Module Slots.
An available Hardpoint if the module is a weapon.
An appropriate Toolkit.
Access to the mech.
Safe Installation
Installing or removing one module under safe shipboard conditions:
Takes one Task.
Costs 1 Energy.
Requires no roll.
Consumes no materials.
Consumes no Toolkit charges.
The module functions immediately after installation.
A character may remove one module and install another as the same Task.
Expedition Installation
Installing or removing a module during an expedition:
Takes one Expedition Turn.
Costs 2 Energy.
Requires an appropriate Toolkit.
Is normally a Skilled Mechanical Technician or Engineering Task.
Suffers −3d6 unless a proper mech berth or portable Workshop is available.
On failure:
The module is not installed.
The Energy and time are spent.
The module remains intact.
The mech may become Disrupted, Power-Starved, or make enough noise to advance a Clock.
Modules cannot normally be installed or removed during combat.

Reconfiguring a Mech
A mech may be reconfigured before each expedition.
Each module swap requires one Task.
If the crew has enough time before departure, several characters may each perform installation Tasks during the same segment.
This allows the crew to adapt its mechs to known threats.
Examples include:
Installing Thermal Shielding before facing Fire creatures.
Equipping Chemical Seals before entering a contaminated laboratory.
Replacing a weapon with a Medical Evacuation Cradle.
Adding Cargo Racks before stripping a derelict.
Installing Electric Resistance after researching a conductive organism.
The crew does not need to permanently commit a mech to one configuration.
Modularity is the mech’s primary advantage.
Damaged Modules
A module may become damaged through:
Monster abilities.
Critical failures.
Mech conditions.
Environmental hazards.
Specific attacks.
The mech reaching 0 HP.
A damaged module provides no benefit until repaired unless its entry says otherwise.
Repairing a module is an ordinary crafting or repair Task.

Deconstructing Modules
A mech module may be deconstructed using the normal rules from Chapter 3.
Routine deconstruction returns approximately 50% of the module’s standard material cost, rounded down.
Because every module of the same Tier has the same cost, all modules of that Tier also provide the same base salvage return.
Unique, biological, or alien components may have additional recovery rules.
Mech Module Summary
Modules use the following simplified procedure:
Choose any standard module.
Pay the universal cost for its Tier.
Spend one crafting Task.
Resolve the Task as Routine or Skilled.
Store the completed module or install it.
Spend one additional Task to install or swap it.
Only the mech frame itself requires a Project.
